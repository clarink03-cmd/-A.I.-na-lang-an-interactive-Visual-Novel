The PremiseThesis, Sections 1.1 and 1.3, and Section 5.2

An awareness tool, not a fix

Over-reliance on AI is a habit shaped by deadlines, grades, and tools that never close. A visual novel cannot undo that. This one does something narrower: it lets a student live through a simulated semester and see, choice by choice, what handing the thinking over costs.

What it does

  • Puts you in campus pressure points: deadlines, quizzes, group work, and citations.
  • Shows the trade-off at once. The grade goes up, Critical Thinking goes down.
  • Keeps a running AI-use count to the end, then explains which of six endings you reached and why.
  • Teaches three Philippine laws (data privacy, cybercrime, copyright) through classmates and professors.

What it does not do

  • Solve, prevent, or reduce AI over-reliance. The study did not test that.
  • Measure your real critical thinking or motivation. The meters are simulation variables.
  • Detect, report, or discipline anyone. It is not a compliance tool and does not replace institutional AI policy.
  • Say AI is bad. The penalty applies to one pattern: AI replacing your own work.

StrengthsThesis, Sections 3.5, 4.1, 4.3, and 4.4

What this project does well

Six strengths of the game and the study. Each one is something you can check in the build or the thesis.

Free, in your browser

No install and no account. It runs on a phone, a tablet, or a PC, and saves stay in your browser with nothing sent to a server. Beta respondents played on Android, iOS, and desktop.

Built from real coursework

Quizzes in Networking, Programming, and Cybersecurity follow the material students actually meet in class, and three Philippine laws (RA 10173, RA 10175, RA 8293) are taught inside the story.

Local and in your language

Set at Mabalacat City College, written in Taglish with a full English mode, with classmates and professors who sound like the people you know.

Rated Excellent on every category

Alpha overall 4.47 from three IT professionals, Beta overall 4.77 from 53 invited MCC students, on ISO/IEC 25010:2023-based instruments. These are perceived-quality ratings. Read how to interpret them.

Open to every player

Three colorblind modes with numbers on the meters, three text sizes, plain-language hints for non-IT players, shuffled answer order, and six save slots.

Designed by the team, with AI as a helper

The characters, interactions, and dialogue were designed and written by the researchers. AI helped with troubleshooting, Python logic, and repetitive code such as screens and menus, and it did not replace the team's own ideas. The code is public and the limits are published here. The ProblemThesis, Chapter 2 (Sections 2.1 to 2.5, 2.10, and 2.11)

Why a game about this

The concern is documented close to home. What is missing is a way for students to feel the consequences themselves.

SourceWhat it reportsRead it with care
Fernando et al. (2026)651 students at accredited colleges in Pampanga reported moderate to high AI dependency, strongest for research and writing.Self-reported, snowball sample. It shows perception, not measured skill loss.
Gumiran and Ambida (2025)Interviews and focus groups with 23 participants at a Philippine state university described academic dishonesty, over-reliance on automated output, and weaker independent analysis.A qualitative single-case study.
Kosmyna et al. (2025)An MIT Media Lab preprint. Essay writers who used an AI chatbot showed weaker neural connectivity than those using a search engine or no tools. It coined "cognitive debt".Small sample, and a commentary urges a more conservative reading. The game's meter is an analogue, not a measurement.
DepEd policyProject AGAP.AI (January 9, 2026) and DepEd Order No. 003, s. 2026 set AI guidance for basic education.They do not cover colleges like MCC. The researchers found no single Philippine statute on AI use in education.

The gap this project fills

None of the reviewed studies tests a game-based intervention on AI misuse among Filipino college students. This project builds one, in the language students actually use, and makes it free to play in a browser. It has not yet tested whether it works.

Sustainable Development Goals

SDG 4 (Quality Education), through responsible technology use and critical thinking. SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure, target 9.b), through local development and open browser-based deployment. This describes direction only, not SDG achievement.

How It WorksThesis, Sections 4.1 and 4.2, and Appendix I (User Manual)

You choose. The meters keep score.

Each quiz offers a way to think it through and a way to skip thinking. Ask the AI and your subject grade rises while Critical Thinking drops by 10. The game adds one to an AI-use count it remembers until the ending. Answer it yourself and you can get it wrong (minus 5 to both), but a correct answer raises both. A Hint button explains the idea without giving away the answer.

Grades start at 65. Below 60 in any subject is an INC (incomplete). Try the trade-off yourself using the values from the Week 3 Python quiz. The mode buttons recolor the meter with the game's own palettes.

The CastThesis, Section 1.3 (Narrative and Content Scope, Character Scope)

One semester, three subjects, ten characters

The story runs through a prologue and eight weekly chapters set in Mabalacat City College, written in Taglish with an English mode. You play Alex or Alexa. Your best friend, Carl or Carly, is chosen in the prologue, and that friend can be a steady companion or a source of peer pressure depending on your choices. Kent, Gabby, and Rey are classmates who act as a moral compass or a peer influence. Each of the ten characters has three expression states.

EvaluationThesis, Sections 3.6 and 4.3 (Tables 8 to 13)

How it was evaluated, and how to read the numbers

Both instruments follow ISO/IEC 25010:2023 software quality characteristics, plus a Content and Educational Effectiveness category. Maintainability was assessed in Alpha only.

Alpha: 3 IT professionals, 59 items, overall mean 4.47 (Excellent). Evaluator means ranged from 4.09 to 4.67.

Beta: 53 invited MCC students, 43 items, overall mean 4.77 (Excellent).

Read the Beta number with care. 41 of the 53 respondents gave one rating to every item. The 12 who varied their answers gave a mean of 4.23. That is still Excellent, but only 0.03 above the 4.20 cutoff, and it is the more conservative figure.

These scores show how evaluators and students perceived the software and its content. They are not evidence that playing the game improves critical thinking or changes AI use.

The BuildThesis, Sections 3.3 to 3.7 and 4.4

How it was built, and the ethics around it

Technology

Written in Ren'Py 8.5.3 and Python, compiled to WebAssembly, and hosted on GitHub Pages at no recurring cost. The game has no server, accounts, or database. Saves stay in your browser.

AI was used to build this game

The story, characters, interactions, dialogue, choices, and scoring logic were designed and written by the researchers, who also wrote a design specification. Several AI tools helped the researchers with troubleshooting, Python logic, and repetitive code such as screens and menus. The lead developer debugged, audited, and revised that code. Every AI-assisted change was reviewed and tested by a person. The full account is in the thesis, Section 3.7. AI helped the researchers and did not replace their ideas.

No generative AI service runs inside the game. The game's lesson is not "never use AI". It is the difference between a tool that speeds you up and one that replaces the learning.

The work followed seven iterations and one post-evaluation revision, not fixed Scrum sprints. The log is in the thesis, with commits as evidence.

Law and data privacy

RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act), RA 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act), and RA 8293 (Intellectual Property Code) appear in the game as fictional scenarios with quizzes. They are not legal advice. The Beta questionnaire was anonymous by default, contact fields were optional, and results are reported as group data.

FAQThesis, Appendix I (User Manual)

Before you play

Do I need to know Filipino?

Dialogue is Taglish, the Tagalog and English mix most students speak. Prefer English? Switch it in Preferences under Wika / Language.

Do I need an IT background?

No. The quizzes cover Networking, Programming, and Cybersecurity, and the Hint button explains the idea behind each question.

Is there a colorblind mode?

Yes. In Preferences, choose Off, Red-Green, or Blue-Yellow. The meter also shows numbers, so color is never the only signal.

Does choosing Ask the AI lock me out of an ending?

No choice stops the story. Your AI-use count and Critical Thinking are two of the values that decide the ending. Zero AI use is one condition for The Real Deal.

Where does my progress go?

Six save slots, stored in your browser on that device. Nothing is sent to a server. Saves are lost if you clear browser data or play in a private window.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes, the same build runs in mobile browsers. Rotate to landscape. The desktop layout is the primary one, and the HUD can overlap on narrow screens.

Does the game prove students think better after playing?

No. The evaluation rated software and content quality and collected self-perceptions. Testing the effect on critical thinking and AI use, with a comparison group or pre and post measures, is listed as future work.

Is the game free?

Yes. It runs in the browser with no download. The first load needs a connection because some assets stream in the background.

Do you know how much you lean on it?

Eight weeks. One semester. A few minutes to find out how the game sees it.

Played it? Share feedback. The formal Beta evaluation (September 15 to 28, 2026) is reported in Chapter 4 of the thesis.
